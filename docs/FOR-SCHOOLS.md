# HW Scan for schools

HW Scan turns photos of homework into one clean PDF. It is free. It works on old phones. After the first visit it works with no internet. No photo ever leaves the phone, so there is no account to make and no student data to protect.

## Set it up once

You need one web address. Any static host will do. GitHub Pages is free and takes ten minutes; the steps are in the main README. Your school's own web server works too, if it uses HTTPS.

Then do three things.

1. Open the address on one phone. Wait for the words "Ready. Works without internet."
2. Turn on airplane mode and reload the page. If the page still opens, offline mode works.
3. Print the address as a QR code and tape it to the wall of the classroom or library.

## Show students how

It takes two minutes. Ask each student to:

1. Open the address on their phone, once, with a connection. School Wi-Fi is fine. The first visit downloads about 1.3 MB. After that, the app uses no data.
2. Tap the browser menu and choose "Add to Home screen." This is optional but it makes the app easy to find.
3. Lay a page flat on a table, under a lamp or by a window. Tap **Take a photo**. Fill the screen with the page.
4. Read what the app says about each page. If it says the photo is blurry or has glare, tap **Retake**.
5. Tap **Make PDF**. The phone offers to share the file, or saves it in Downloads.

Tell students to put their name in the file name. Open **Settings** to do it. The phone remembers it.

## What else it does

Students who want more can tap **Edit** on any page. They can:

- Choose colour, gray, or black and white, and make the page lighter or darker.
- Drag the four corners to fix a page the app cropped wrongly.
- Turn the page, or use the whole photo.

In **Settings**, open **Show advanced options** to change paper size (Letter, A4, or match each page), file size, the look of every page, and whether the text should be searchable. A searchable PDF can be searched and read aloud by a screen reader. It needs about 5 more megabytes the first time, then works offline.

If the browser closes, or the phone restarts the page after the camera, the pages are still there. The app saves them on the phone.

## What works and what does not

It works best on a dark table under even light. It copes with shadows, slight tilt, and cheap cameras.

It struggles with a white page on a white table. When it cannot find the edges, it says so. The student should move the page to a darker surface and try again.

Printed worksheets and pencil handwriting come out well. Very faint pencil may need a brighter room.

## Phones that work

Any phone from about 2016 on, with a current browser: Chrome, Firefox, Safari, Samsung Internet. The app limits photo size so that phones with 2 GB of memory do not run out.

If a student has no phone, a school laptop with a webcam can use **Choose photos** on pictures taken any other way.

## Updating

When the app changes, the host copies the new files. Phones fetch them the next time they are online. Students do nothing.

## Privacy

The app has no server, no tracking and no account. It keeps the student's settings and unfinished pages on the phone, and nowhere else. **Settings ??? Erase everything saved on this phone** removes them. Clearing the browser's data does the same. On a shared phone or school device, tell students to erase when they finish.
